Privacy Policy

This policy explains how Https Browser handles information when you use the Android app. It is written to describe the app's current implementation, including information kept on your device and information that websites you visit may receive.

App: Https Browser Publisher: MD.Nirob · RootDeck Last updated: 18 November 2026 Application ID: com.rootdeck.https_browser
In short
  • The reviewed app stores browser information such as history, bookmarks, tabs, sessions and reading-list items locally on your device.
  • The reviewed source contains no app account system, advertising SDK, analytics SDK or crash-reporting service.
  • Websites and search providers you choose to use receive the information needed to provide their services, such as your IP address, requested URLs and applicable cookies.
  • Https Browser is intended for a broad audience, including younger learners, but it is not a parental-control or certified child-safe browser.

1. Overview and scope

Https Browser is an Android web browser published by MD.Nirob under the RootDeck project. This policy describes the app source reviewed for version 1.0, application ID com.rootdeck.https_browser.

The app is designed with users including children and teenagers aged 5–16 in mind. Because a browser connects to websites operated by other organizations, this policy distinguishes between information handled by Https Browser and information collected independently by the websites, search engines, Android operating system, or other services you choose to use.

This policy may not describe modified, unofficial, or future versions of the app. If a future release adds accounts, cloud synchronization, analytics, advertising, crash reporting, or other network services, this policy must be updated before that release is distributed.

2. Information Https Browser processes

The reviewed app processes the following categories of information to provide browser features:

CategoryExamplesHow it is used
Browsing historyPage URLs, page titles and visit timestampsTo show and manage browsing history. Normal history entries are not added for pages opened in the app's incognito mode.
Tabs and sessionsOpen tab URLs, titles, tab order and saved session detailsTo restore tabs and saved browsing sessions.
BookmarksSaved page URLs, titles and save timestampsTo let you return to pages.
Reading listPage URL, title, excerpt and extracted article textTo save articles for later or offline reading.
Website storageCookies, local storage, session storage and cached website dataTo support website sign-in, preferences and normal website operation. Websites may set their own cookies, including third-party cookies.
Temporary diagnosticsNetwork request URLs, methods, request headers, cookie values and timing metadataTo display browser network activity in the in-app developer/network inspection tools while the app is running.

The reviewed source does not show an app-operated account-registration system or a developer server receiving the locally stored browsing history, bookmarks, reading list or saved sessions. It also does not include an analytics, advertising or crash-reporting SDK in the reviewed build configuration.

Https Browser does not need you to provide your name, email address, phone number or date of birth to use its browser features. If you enter personal information into a website, that information is handled by that website under its own terms and privacy policy.

3. Information stored on your device

Browser records are stored in the app's local database and app-specific storage. This includes history, bookmarks, tabs, saved sessions, reading-list content and temporary page files. Website cookies and web storage are maintained by Android WebView for the sites you visit.

Https Browser enables JavaScript, DOM storage, form-data support and third-party cookies in its WebView configuration. These features allow websites to work as expected, but also mean websites may store identifiers, preferences or other information in browser storage. The app also uses Android's autofill integration points; any autofill service or password manager on your device is governed by that provider's own privacy terms.

The reviewed app does not provide a separate cloud password vault. Do not assume that credentials entered on websites are stored or protected by Https Browser itself.

4. Websites, search providers and third parties

When you open a website, that website and its service providers may receive information such as your device's IP address, requested page and resource URLs, browser and device information included in standard web requests, request headers, and cookies applicable to that site. They may use this information for their own functionality, analytics, advertising, security or personalization.

The app's address/search handling uses Google Search for searches entered as search terms. If you submit a search, the search terms are sent to Google, and Google's privacy policy applies to that activity. You may also choose to visit other search engines and websites.

Https Browser does not control third-party websites, their cookies, their security practices, or how long they retain information. Review the privacy policy of each website or service you use. A website may display advertisements even though the reviewed Https Browser app itself does not include an advertising SDK.

The app includes a locally bundled tracker-domain blocklist. When tracking protection is enabled, the browser checks requested hostnames against that local list and blocks matching requests on the device. This check does not require sending your browsing history to RootDeck, and it does not guarantee that all tracking is blocked.

5. Developer tools and network inspection

Https Browser includes developer and network inspection screens. While the app is running, the network inspector records request metadata in app memory. Depending on the request, this can include the URL, HTTP method, request headers and cookie values, plus timing and blocking information.

Important Request headers and cookies can contain sensitive information, including session identifiers. Do not share screenshots, exports, logs or screen recordings from the network inspector unless you have checked them and removed private information.

In the reviewed implementation, these inspection records are used for the in-app developer tools and are not sent to a RootDeck analytics or diagnostics server. They are temporary app-memory data and may be cleared when the app or inspector state is reset.

6. Android permissions and device access

The reviewed Android manifest declares these permissions:

  • Internet: required to load websites and other online content.
  • Network state: used to check network connectivity.
  • Read external storage (Android 12L / API 32 and earlier only): declared for legacy file access on older Android versions. Android's file and media access behavior varies by OS version.

The reviewed manifest does not declare permissions for precise location, camera, microphone or contacts. Websites may still ask for information directly through their own pages; do not provide it unless you trust the website.

The app can open supported local HTML, MHTML and PDF files shared with it. Be cautious with files from unknown sources. The browser's local-file viewing features may grant local documents access needed to render their content.

7. Children and teen users

Https Browser is designed to be useful to a broad learning audience, including ages 5–16. Younger users should browse with a parent, guardian or teacher who can help choose suitable websites and explain safe online behavior.

The reviewed app does not include child accounts, age verification, parental controls, website-category filtering, or guaranteed safe-search enforcement. It is not certified as a child-safe browser. Websites visited through the app may independently collect personal information, show advertisements, or provide content that is not suitable for every age.

Parents and guardians should supervise browsing, review the privacy practices of websites children use, and avoid allowing children to share personal details with unknown websites. If you believe a child has provided personal information to the app publisher directly, contact the publisher using the contact details below.

8. Your choices and deleting information

You can manage some information from within the app:

  • Browsing history and page files: use the app's clear-browsing-data control. The reviewed implementation clears the history database and saved page files.
  • Cookies and website storage: use the clear-site-data control. The reviewed implementation removes WebView cookies and Web Storage data.
  • Bookmarks, reading-list entries and saved sessions: remove these from their respective app screens. The general clear-browsing-data function does not currently delete all of these records.
  • Network inspector records: clear the network panel or close/reset the relevant app state.
  • Exported files: delete PDFs, page captures or other files you saved outside the app using the file manager or gallery where they were saved.

Clearing information in Https Browser does not delete information held by websites, search providers, Android autofill services, other apps, or previously created device backups. You may need to sign out of websites and use their own account/privacy tools to remove data stored on their servers.

Uninstalling the app normally removes its app-private local data from the device. This may not remove files you exported elsewhere or information that was included in an Android/device backup.

9. Security and safe browsing

Browser data is kept in Android app storage and WebView storage on the device. This policy does not claim that every local database, cache file or website record is separately encrypted by Https Browser. Protect your device with an appropriate screen lock and keep Android and Android System WebView up to date.

Https Browser can open both HTTPS and HTTP websites. HTTPS helps protect data in transit between your device and a website, but it does not make a website trustworthy or safe. Avoid entering sensitive information on pages that do not use HTTPS, and leave unfamiliar or suspicious websites.

Websites run their own scripts inside the browser. Since JavaScript and third-party cookies are enabled in the reviewed configuration, only visit sites you trust.

10. Retention, backups and storage limits

Local browser records generally remain on the device until you delete them, clear the relevant browser data, the app replaces them, or Android removes temporary/cache files. Some temporary page captures and PDF files are stored in the app cache and may be removed by Android when storage is needed.

The app manifest has Android backup enabled. Whether app data is included in a device or cloud backup depends on Android version, device configuration and the user's backup settings. If you use Android backup, browser data may be restored when the app is reinstalled. Check your device's backup settings if you do not want app data backed up.

RootDeck does not set a separate server-side retention period for browsing records in the reviewed implementation because the app does not appear to upload those records to a publisher-operated service.

If a future version introduces a new type of data collection or sharing, the policy should explain that change before the updated version is released.
